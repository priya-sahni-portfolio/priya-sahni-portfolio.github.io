Method & assumptions

Six fictional job groups. Each worker is assigned 1,800 hours for a full year. Rates, headcounts and benchmark rates are editable in the workbook. Only positive above-market gaps are reduced; rates at or below market stay flat.

Gap % = current rate / benchmark − 1 Target = current − MAX(current − benchmark, 0) × closure % Annual spend = headcount × annual hours × rate Potential saving = current spend − proposed spend

Interpretation

At 50% gap closure, the model identifies CAD 315,000 in potential annual savings, or 2.27% of baseline spend. This is a mathematical scenario, not realized savings.

Start discussions with above-market groups. Validate title matching, seniority, geography, service quality and supplier contract terms. Below-market rates may need a separate talent-supply assessment.

Limitations: no taxes, fees, implementation costs, headcount changes or supplier acceptance assumptions are included. The slider changes this page only; the downloaded workbook opens at 50%.

Data-quality and model checks

One row per fictional role. Benchmarks and hours are positive. Summary totals reconcile to row calculations. Zero gap closure produces zero savings; full gap closure produces CAD 630,000. The workbook’s base and boundary cases were recalculated during creation.

02 / SOURCED RESEARCH FRAMEWORK · FICTIONAL ASSET COMPARISON

Oncology intelligence brief

Decision question: how should a strategy team organize pipeline information and prioritize follow-up research without confusing development activity with evidence of treatment benefit?

Public-source research findings

Clinical-trial phases answer different questions. Early studies examine safety and dosing; later comparative studies assess a treatment against a comparator. Development stage should therefore be tracked separately from reported outcomes. [1]

“Active, not recruiting” means a study continues but is not enrolling new participants. Recruitment status alone does not establish efficacy or regulatory approval. [2]

Analytical implication

Compare assets within a clearly defined indication, patient population and treatment setting. Capture phase, status, comparator, endpoints, source date and reported evidence as separate fields.

My synthesis: use upcoming evidence events to prioritize monitoring. Avoid ranking treatments from phase or recruitment status alone, and avoid unsupported cross-trial efficacy comparisons.

Recommended monitoring plan

Maintain one source log with study ID, URL, last verification date and changed fields. Review registry updates monthly and material disclosures as released. Investigate changes to endpoints, enrollment and timelines, then publish a short “what changed / why it matters / what remains unknown” update.

Deliverable: a structured pipeline tracker and a one-page briefing. This example demonstrates the method; it does not claim a complete or current oncology market landscape.

03 / ILLUSTRATIVE PROPOSAL · NOT AN IMPLEMENTED CLIENT PROJECT

A practical nonprofit AI pilot

Scenario: a small nonprofit wants to reduce the effort of researching publicly available funding opportunities and drafting outreach. This is a proposed four-week pilot using an illustrative organization.

Use case 1

Research summaries

Summarize a supplied public funder page into eligibility, geography, deadline and required documents. Every field links back to its source.

Use case 2

Outreach first drafts

Draft an introduction from approved organizational facts and verified funder priorities. A staff member checks and approves every message.

Use case 3

Internal knowledge support

Organize approved public FAQs and program descriptions into a searchable reference. Start with a small, maintained content set.

Four-week rollout proposal

Week 1 · Define & measure

Program lead selects one workflow and ten public-source tasks. Record existing completion time, required checks and source quality. Assign an owner.

Week 2 · Design & test

Build a prompt template and source checklist. Run a small test with public or synthetic information. Record omissions and invented statements.

Week 3 · Pilot with review

Compare assisted and manual work on matched tasks. Include fact-checking, corrections and review in total time. Staff approve outputs.

Week 4 · Decide & document

Review quality and effort against the proposed criteria. Expand only if results support it. Create an owner guide and a process for retiring outdated content.

Proposed evaluation criteria

These are pilot targets, not achieved results:

  • At least 20% lower median total task time, including review and corrections.
  • All critical eligibility, deadline and amount fields verified against original sources.
  • No unsupported claims in the approved final output.
  • No personal beneficiary or donor information in test prompts.

Tools & responsibilities

Excel or SharePoint for the source log; an organization-approved AI tool for draft assistance. The program lead owns selection, a staff reviewer owns verification, and the operations lead owns documentation.

Decision rule

If time improves but quality declines, revise the workflow and retest. If neither improves, stop the pilot. Licensing and implementation costs must be assessed before expansion.

Example prompt template
Using only the public funder text below, extract: 1. Eligible organizations and locations 2. Funding amount, if explicitly stated 3. Deadline and timezone, if stated 4. Required submission materials For every field, provide a source excerpt or section reference. If information is absent or conflicting, write “Needs verification.” Do not infer eligibility and do not draft a claim about our organization. Public source text: [insert verified page text]
